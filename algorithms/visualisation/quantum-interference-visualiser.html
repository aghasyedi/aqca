Quantum Interference Visualiser

A real two-qubit interferometer: a particle qubit plus a which-path detector. Watch the two path amplitudes add as phasors, and watch the fringes wash out as path information grows.

1. Interferometer

0°
0°

θ rotates an internal "path detector" qubit. At θ = 0 the detector cannot distinguish the paths and interference is perfect. At θ = 180° the detector states are orthogonal, the paths are fully labelled and the fringes vanish.

D² + V² = 1 is the Englert–Greenberger–Yasin duality relation: you may know which path the particle took, or you may see interference — never both at full strength.

Interference Fringe

P(0) = ½(1 + V cos φ). As the which-path marking grows, V falls and the fringes flatten towards the classical ½ / ½ mixture.

Amplitudes Stage By Stage

Watch the |10⟩ and |11⟩ entries: the phase φ only rotates them, while θ moves amplitude from |10⟩ into |11⟩ — the component that can no longer interfere.
