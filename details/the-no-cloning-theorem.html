The No-Cloning Theorem

1. Statement of the Theorem

Published in 1982 by Wootters, Zurek, and Dieks, the No-Cloning Theorem is a profound no-go theorem in quantum mechanics. It states that it is physically impossible to create an independent and identical copy of an arbitrary, unknown quantum state.

Unlike classical data, which can be effortlessly copied from one hard drive to another, quantum information is fundamentally fragile and unique.

2. The Wootters-Zurek Proof

The proof relies on two absolute pillars of quantum mechanics: Unitary evolution and Linearity. We proceed by contradiction.

Assume there exists a "Universal Quantum Copier", described by a unitary matrix \(U\). It takes two inputs: the unknown state \(|\psi\rangle\) that we want to copy, and a blank "scratchpad" qubit initialized to \(|0\rangle\). After applying \(U\), the machine successfully copies the state:

\(U(|\psi\rangle \otimes |0\rangle) = |\psi\rangle \otimes |\psi\rangle\)

Let's say this machine is capable of copying two distinct, arbitrary orthogonal states, \(|\phi\rangle\) and \(|\varphi\rangle\):

\(U(|\phi\rangle |0\rangle) = |\phi\rangle |\phi\rangle\)

\(U(|\varphi\rangle |0\rangle) = |\varphi\rangle |\varphi\rangle\)

Now, suppose we input a superposition of these two states, \(|\alpha\rangle = a|\phi\rangle + b|\varphi\rangle\). Because quantum operators must be linear, the operation of \(U\) on the superposition must equal the sum of its operations on the individual basis vectors:

\(U(|\alpha\rangle |0\rangle) = U \Big( (a|\phi\rangle + b|\varphi\rangle) \otimes |0\rangle \Big)\)

\(= a \, U(|\phi\rangle|0\rangle) + b \, U(|\varphi\rangle|0\rangle)\)

\(= a |\phi\rangle|\phi\rangle + b |\varphi\rangle|\varphi\rangle\)

However, if the machine actually cloned the state \(|\alpha\rangle\), the output should physically be two independent copies of \(|\alpha\rangle\):

\(|\alpha\rangle \otimes |\alpha\rangle = (a|\phi\rangle + b|\varphi\rangle) \otimes (a|\phi\rangle + b|\varphi\rangle)\)

\(= a^2 |\phi\rangle|\phi\rangle + ab |\phi\rangle|\varphi\rangle + ba |\varphi\rangle|\phi\rangle + b^2 |\varphi\rangle|\varphi\rangle\)

The Contradiction

For these two expressions to be equal (i.e., for the copier to actually work), the cross terms \(ab\) must be zero. This is only true if either \(a=0\) or \(b=0\), meaning the state was not a superposition at all! Therefore, a universal cloning machine violates the linearity of quantum mechanics and cannot exist.

3. Implications and the No-Deleting Theorem

Because you cannot copy an unknown quantum state, classical error correction (which relies on backing up data) is impossible. This led to the development of highly complex Logical Qubits to protect data using entanglement instead of copying.

Furthermore, in 2000, the corollary No-Deleting Theorem was proven: it is equally impossible to take two identical unknown quantum states and delete one of them to return the scratchpad to a pristine \(|0\rangle\) state. Quantum information is strictly conserved.
