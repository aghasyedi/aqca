Fidelity

1. What is Fidelity?

In classical statistics, if you have two probability distributions, you can calculate their overlap using metrics like the Bhattacharyya distance. In quantum information theory, we need a way to measure the "closeness" or "overlap" of two quantum states, described by density matrices \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\).

This metric is called Fidelity. It is heavily utilized by experimentalists during Quantum State Tomography to report how well their noisy hardware actually executed the ideal mathematical circuit.

2. The Generalized Uhlmann-Jozsa Definition

For two arbitrary mixed states (density matrices \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\)), the fidelity is defined by a somewhat intimidating matrix equation:

\(F(\rho, \sigma) = \left( \text{Tr} \sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}} \right)^2\)

Note: In some textbooks (like Nielsen & Chuang), fidelity is defined without the square. We use the squared version here as it represents probabilities more intuitively.

This equation is complex because matrices generally do not commute (\(\rho\sigma \neq \sigma\rho\)). Taking the square root of a matrix involves finding its eigenvectors, taking the square root of its eigenvalues, and reconstructing it.

3. Crucial Simplifications

Thankfully, in almost all practical laboratory applications, we are comparing a messy, experimental mixed state (\(\rho\)) against a mathematically perfect, ideal pure state (\(\sigma = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\)).

When at least one of the states is pure, the square roots completely collapse, and the fidelity simplifies to the expectation value of the density matrix in the ideal state:

\(F(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|, \rho) = \langle \psi | \rho | \psi \rangle\)

If both states are pure (\(|\psi\rangle\) and \(|\phi\rangle\)), the formula simplifies even further to just the squared magnitude of their inner product—the exact same equation as the Born Rule for Measurement!

\(F(|\psi\rangle, |\phi\rangle) = |\langle \psi | \phi \rangle|^2\)

4. Fundamental Properties

Fidelity satisfies several intuitive mathematical properties:

  • Boundedness: \(0 \le F(\rho, \sigma) \le 1\). It is a probability-like measure.
  • Identity (Maximum): \(F(\rho, \sigma) = 1\) if and only if \(\rho = \sigma\). The states are perfectly identical.
  • Orthogonality (Minimum): \(F(\rho, \sigma) = 0\) if the states have absolutely zero overlap (they exist in orthogonal subspaces).
  • Symmetry: \(F(\rho, \sigma) = F(\sigma, \rho)\). The closeness of A to B is the same as B to A.
  • Unitary Invariance: \(F(U\rho U^\dagger, U\sigma U^\dagger) = F(\rho, \sigma)\). Rotating both states by the same quantum gate does not change the distance between them.

While Fidelity measures "closeness", its dual metric, Trace Distance, measures "distinguishability."
